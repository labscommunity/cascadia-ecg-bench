Serving Qwen MoE models on Intel Panther Lake AI PCs: whole, sharded, and what the memory window changes

Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and Qwen3.5-122B-A10B measured on three 32 GB Core Ultra X7 358H NUCs and one 64 GB X7 358H in Intel Tiber: time to first token, decode speed, package power, CPU / iGPU / NPU and memory utilization, output parity and task accuracy, for every layout the hardware allows. Covers the GPU memory window setting the 32 GB boxes run with, how the 122B IR is built, speculative decoding, GPU clocks, and a per-stage profile of the pipeline.

What the measurements say

Memory windowA 32 GB Panther Lake box serves the 35B whole at 33 tok/s

With the Windows GPU commit limit at 85% (one dxgkrnl registry value; Intel's INF installs 57%) the iGPU may keep 26.1 GiB resident, the 19.4 GB model is fully resident, and one NUC decodes at 33 tok/s with 0.75 s TTFT at 1k tokens, byte-identical to the 64 GB box's output. Every 32 GB box in this report runs with that setting.

ShardingSharding across two or three NUCs works and costs prefill, not accuracy

18–19 tok/s per stream, 3.5 s to first token on a 1k prompt, 6–9 GB GPU memory and 9–15 W per NUC, same GSM8K accuracy as the whole model. TTFT on a 7.7k prompt goes from 2.9 s whole to 22–31 s across NUCs.

64 GB nodeOne 64 GB box is the faster, leaner unit for the 35B

Whole model: 42–48 tok/s, 0.6 s TTFT, 34 W, 1.23 tok/J, and linear multi-stream scaling to 132 tok/s at 8 streams. Sharding on that box costs 35% of decode and 2.6x TTFT.

122BThe 122B runs all-local on three NUCs at 10.6 tok/s

No public IR exists; the runtime's GPTQ-to-OpenVINO builder produces a 64.3 GB INT4 IR when run with --expert-sym (the GPU plugin's MoE fusion needs signed experts). Cut into 20.7 / 24.7 / 25.0 GB stages, it fits the three NUCs' 26.1 GiB budgets and decodes at 10.6 tok/s with the ranks' GPU keep-alive on; 5.5 s TTFT at 1k tokens, 58 W. A 64 GB tail over the Tiber relay runs the same answers at 1.2–2.7 tok/s.

Where the 122B's time goesA 122B token is three 32 ms stage steps and two LAN hops, and the ranks must not idle between them

With the three NUCs' GPU keep-alive on, each rank's stage step is 32 ms median (p95 33 ms), the two LAN hops cost about 2 ms each, and a token takes ~95 ms. Without the keep-alive the iGPU idles between hops and the driver charges ~0.5 s on 40–50% of steps, so the ranks are run with CASCADIA_QWEN36_GPU_KEEPALIVE_MS=2.

Speculative decodingSpeculative decoding does not pay on these GPUs: prompt lookup costs 35% on the whole 35B, n-gram speculation halves the staged 35B

OpenVINO GenAI's prompt-lookup path (continuous batching) drops the whole 35B from 47 to 31–32 tok/s and doubles TTFT; a draft model is refused on VLM-layout exports, which every Qwen3.6 IR here is. In the staged engine, greedy n-gram speculation with DeltaNet state snapshot/rollback runs the single-box 35B at 14–15 tok/s against 31.6 plain: 2–20% draft acceptance and 120 ms per state snapshot. On the all-local 122B the same path runs at 0.55x the plain rate (acceptance 14–23%), because every rollback is paid on all three ranks in sequence. Output parity with plain decoding held on 4 of 6 outputs; the rest differ by a character or two (f16 verify-vs-decode).

GPU clocksGPU clocks are not a lever: pinning the iGPU at 2.5 GHz or raising the OpenVINO queue priority changes nothing

During decode the iGPU's frequency spans 100 to 2,500 MHz. Pinning it at 2,500 MHz through Level Zero Sysman, or compiling with GPU_QUEUE_THROTTLE/PRIORITY=HIGH, leaves the 122B chain at 10.5 tok/s and the whole 35B on a NUC at 33 tok/s. The Intel graphics power plan is Maximum Performance on every box.

Device mixCPU decode is a 2x loss, the NPU does not load these models, HETERO changes nothing

Dense Qwen3-8B on one 32 GB box: 22 tok/s GPU vs 11 CPU, 1k-token prefill 0.3 s vs 22 s. A 35B shard pair with its tail on a CPU halves decode and makes long-prompt decode 5x slower. Neither the 8B nor the 0.6B compiles for the NPU with OpenVINO 2026.4.1, and no engine splits prefill and decode across devices.

AccuracyAccuracy survives every layout; token parity holds only for the same IR on a GPU

GSM8K: whole 35B 37/40, sharded 36/40, 122B 17/20 (all misses 400-token truncations; the 35B scored 18–19/20 on the same questions). The whole model is byte-identical across the 64 GB box and the 32 GB NUC; sharded output diverges after the first sentence (f16 near-ties) without changing task scores.

Hardware, memory, and the window

Every box is an Intel Core Ultra X7 358H (4P + 8E + 4LPE cores, Arc B390 iGPU). What bounds a model on a box is not RAM but the share of it the Windows video memory manager lets the iGPU keep resident: the per-process budget the OS derives from the WDDM shared segment. The three NUCs run with the commit limit at 85%; delta is at the driver default.

The setting

The segment the OS enforces comes from HKLM\SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Control\GraphicsDrivers\MemoryManager\SystemPartitionCommitLimitPercentage (Intel's INF installs 57; Intel Graphics Software's "Shared GPU Memory Override" slider writes it, together with the driver's own GMM\GpuSystemMemoryPinninglimit). The per-process budget is the segment minus 0.75 GiB on every box measured. The GMM key on its own only changes what OpenVINO reports as GPU_DEVICE_TOTAL_MEM_SIZE; the dxgkrnl key is the one that sets residency. Both are set to 85 on the NUCs:

reg add "HKLM\SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Control\GraphicsDrivers\MemoryManager" ^
  /v SystemPartitionCommitLimitPercentage /t REG_DWORD /d 85 /f
reg add "HKLM\SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Control\Class\{4d36e968-e325-11ce-bfc1-08002be10318}\0000\GMM" ^
  /v GpuSystemMemoryPinninglimit /t REG_DWORD /d 85 /f
shutdown /r /t 5

Qwen3.5-122B-A10B

Built. No public OpenVINO IR exists. From Qwen/Qwen3.5-122B-A10B-GPTQ-Int4 (79 GB) the runtime's GPTQ-to-OpenVINO builder produced a 64.3 GB INT4 IR (21.5 min on 8 vCPUs), cut into 2, 3 and 4 stages. The 2-stage cut passed the exporter's chain-vs-full check (logits rel 2.0e-2, top-1 match, top-5 4/5, 8/8 greedy tokens).

Build requirement. The GPU plugin's MoE fusion (OpenVINO 2026.4.1) rejects expert weights with a uniform zero point, which is what a GPTQ checkpoint produces (symmetric, every zero point 8), so the builder is run with --expert-sym: the expert stacks are emitted as signed INT4 without a zero point (bit-identical values), which the GPU compiles. The 35B rebuilt the same way runs at 53 tok/s on the 64 GB box, 9% faster than Intel's own export at equal accuracy (A6).

Sized. 64.3 GB fits no single Panther Lake box: the 64 GB node's budget is 51.2 GiB and the 32 GB nodes' 26.1 GiB. The all-local layout is three NUC stages of 20.7 / 24.7 / 25.0 GB; the alternative is three NUC stages of 13 / 16 / 16 GB plus a 26 GB tail on the 64 GB node, which crosses the NUC-to-Tiber relay twice per token.

The local and the relay layouts produce token-identical answers (the same completion tokens on GSM8K-20) and differ only in speed: the local chain decodes at 10.6 tok/s, the relay chain at 1.2–2.7 because each token crosses the Tiber relay twice. The per-stage profile in the clocks section shows what a local token consists of.

Expert parallelism does not help this case. A token touches 8 of 256 experts per layer; spreading them over three boxes saves at most two thirds of a ~1 ms matvec per layer and pays two network exchanges per layer (96 per token) for it. Layer pipelining costs two exchanges per token in total, and the 35B pipeline shows those are worth about 25 ms. Expert sharding pays off with large batches and a sub-100 µs interconnect, neither of which a single-stream edge deployment has.

Speculative decoding

Two kinds were added and measured. For the whole model, OpenVINO GenAI's own speculative paths (prompt lookup, and FastDraft with a separate draft model) were enabled through the runtime's --prompt-lookup / --draft-model flags; GenAI 2026.4.1 refuses them with prefix caching on, so the shim now passes a scheduler config with prefix caching disabled for those pipelines. For the staged pipeline engine, greedy n-gram (prompt-lookup) speculation was implemented from scratch on branch ecg/spec-decode: the head drafts up to k tokens from n-gram matches in the prompt and generated text, the chain verifies them in one T=k+1 pass (the last stage now emits logits for every position, cascadia shard --full-logits), rank 0 accepts the longest matching prefix, and because the Gated DeltaNet recurrent state cannot be rewound, every rank snapshots its DeltaNet variables before the verify pass and, on a partial accept, restores them, masks the rejected cache slots and re-feeds the accepted tokens. New wire frames VERIFY / TOKENS / COMMIT carry this through the pipeline.

Across three ranks one verify step costs a 0.37 s DeltaNet snapshot, a 0.73 s verify pass, a 0.75 s restore and a 0.84 s re-feed, against a 0.33 s plain token; the pipeline multiplies every rollback cost by its depth.

The staged implementation is correct to the extent greedy speculation can be on this export: short outputs are byte-identical to plain decoding, the longer ones differ by one or two characters, the same f16 near-tie behaviour the surgery documents for any T>1 pass versus T=1 decode on the DeltaNet scan. It is not a win on this hardware for two structural reasons: prompt-lookup drafts rarely match the model on prose and telemetry (2–20% accepted), and the recurrent state makes rollback expensive (a snapshot/restore round trip costs more than three plain tokens). A learned draft (the MTP head that ships in these IRs, or a 0.8B draft on a text-only export) would raise acceptance, but the rollback cost stays unless the verify can run on a cloned state; that is the next experiment, not this one. The flags remain in the branch, off by default.

The runtime branch adds per-token instrumentation (CASCADIA_QWEN36_PROFILE=1: embed, per-stage set/infer/output, transport wait, sampling; and an OpenVINO per-op dump with PERF_COUNT). On the all-local 122B, per rank and decode step:

A token is three stage steps in sequence plus two hops: about 95 ms. The ranks run with CASCADIA_QWEN36_GPU_KEEPALIVE_MS=2, which keeps each device busy with a tiny synthetic model while it waits on the network. This is required on these iGPUs: a device that idles for tens of milliseconds while holding 20–25 GB is charged about 0.5 s on 40–50% of its next steps (a standalone stage shows the same: p99 57 ms with no pause between steps, 16% of steps at ~570 ms with a 70 ms pause, p99 68 ms with keep-alive inferences in the pause). A 2 ms period keeps both the device and its clock up; at 5 ms the clock settles low unless pinned, at 25 ms every step runs at a low clock (117 ms). The keep-alive is why the chain draws 58 W and why the GPU busy counters read 93–95%: the keep-alive kernels fill the waits.

Of a 51 ms standalone step, OpenVINO's per-op profile attributes 18–22 ms to kernels (149 compressed matmuls 13–17 ms, transposes 2.4 ms, 12 Gated DeltaNet ops 1.1 ms; the fused MoE experts are not attributed), so roughly 30 ms per step is the plugin's host-side dispatch across ~1,100 nodes of a dynamic-shape graph. The first requests after a load are slower (14–18 s for 16 tokens) while kernels are specialised for new prompt lengths; a persistent OpenVINO cache directory does not change the steady-state step.

What this means for ECG deployments

  • Provision every 32 GB box with the GPU commit limit at 85%. One registry value and a reboot; with it a 32 GB Panther Lake node is a 33 tok/s single-stream server for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, byte-identical to the 64 GB box's output.
  • With the window set, one 32 GB node per stream is the unit for the 35B. It runs at 70% of the 64 GB box's speed at the same power, and three such nodes serving three independent streams beat one 3-NUC sharded chain serving one stream by 5x in aggregate throughput.
  • Shard when the model does not fit, not to go faster. Sharding across NUCs costs 55% of decode and 4–11x of TTFT and keeps accuracy. It is what makes the 122B run on three ordinary NUCs, and what lets a box hold a second model beside the first.
  • The 122B is a selective upgrade, not a general one. It scored the same as the 35B on GSM8K-20 at three times the per-token cost of the 35B chain (10.6 vs 18 tok/s on the same three NUCs, at twice the power). Default to Qwen3.6-35B-A3B; reserve the 122B for knowledge-heavy or long-video tasks where the vendor's benchmarks show a gap.
  • Design around prefill. Long prompts cost 4–11x more TTFT when staged. Feed per-minute telemetry windows or pre-summarized context rather than hour-long dumps.
  • Keep shard links on a local 2.5 GbE segment. A 15 ms hop is tolerable; the 110–350 ms NUC-to-cloud path makes a 122B chain relay-bound at 1–3 tok/s regardless of the GPUs.
  • The NPU plays no part in LLM serving today and there is no NPU-prefill / GPU-decode split to be had; NPU-capable SKUs gain nothing here. CPU decode is a 2x loss on a dense 8B and worse on a 35B shard.
  • Use OVMS continuous batching on 64 GB nodes when several users share one box: 4x the tokens per joule at 8 streams.
  • Run pipeline ranks with the GPU keep-alive on (CASCADIA_QWEN36_GPU_KEEPALIVE_MS=2): a rank that waits on other boxes must not let its iGPU idle. It costs nothing when the rank is not waiting. Do not spend effort on GPU clock settings.
  • Leave speculative decoding off on Panther Lake iGPUs for these models. Prompt lookup costs a third of the whole model's speed and n-gram speculation halves the staged engine; the DeltaNet rollback cost and low n-gram acceptance decide it. Revisit only with the MTP head or a text-only draft export.

Method, scope, and corrections

  • Whole model: cascadia run --engine ov-genai --device GPU. Shards: cascadia shard (IR surgery at layer boundaries, byte-identical manifests verified on every rank) then one cascadia worker --engine qwen35 --rank r --total N per box. Client on the serving box over loopback; 1 warmup, 3 repeats, medians (quick runs: 2 repeats). The ov-genai engine returns each completion as a single stream chunk, hence the two-call TTFT/decode method; the staged engine streams per token.
  • Power and DDR bandwidth: Intel SoC Watch (driver 2.20.5), 60 s window during a continuous decode loop and 30 s idle with the model loaded, on every rank of a chain. Utilization: 1 s Windows performance-counter sampler with GPU engines attributed per adapter. Memory budgets: D3DKMTQueryVideoMemoryInfo; GPU frequency: Level Zero Sysman (zesFrequencyGetState).
  • The 122B IR was built with the runtime's GPTQ-to-OpenVINO builder; its --expert-sym option is the fix described in §4. Stage trees were cut on an 8-vCPU VM and distributed over the LAN; every rank's manifest hash and stage byte counts were checked before launch.
  • Machines were borrowed and restored: the honyaku ranks and watchdogs on alpha/beta, the Acrow node on charlie, the enterprise node service on tate-07. The commit-limit change is left enabled on the three NUCs. The runtime build with the keep-alive and speculative paths is at C:\ecgbench\rt\spec on every box; the 122B stage trees were removed from alpha and beta to give their services disk back (re-staged from the Mac Pro in minutes), charlie and tate-07 keep theirs.
